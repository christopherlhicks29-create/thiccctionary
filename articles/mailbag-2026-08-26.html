Filed Replies, Vol. 13

Bartholomew Whitmore, Senior Cataloguer

From T. Okafor, Columbus:

I submitted a photo of my neighbor's above-ground pool three months ago and never heard back. It's very large. Is that not exactly what you're looking for?

*Submitted via web form, August 2026*

The photograph, whatever its merits, is not a submission. A photograph of a thing is evidence, not documentation; the distinction is in Section II of the Style Guide and has been since 1979. What I require is a completed Subject Profile, a measurement declaration witnessed by a notary or a credible equivalent, and a silhouette rendering as specified under Amendment 1991-08. "Very large" is not a measurement. "My neighbor's" is not provenance. Filed a procedural note to the submissions desk. Start over.

From P. Crenshaw, Albuquerque:

You catalogued a mining truck in 1986. A mining truck. I'm submitting a genuine 14-foot inflatable turkey for Thanksgiving and you're telling me it doesn't qualify? The turkey is, objectively, thiccc.

*Letter received by post, August 2026*

The 1986 Liebherr T 282B entry (/about/documents/liebherr-memo/) passed the Silhouette Test under criteria that did not yet formally exist, because I wrote the Silhouette Test three years later partly in response to the arguments the Liebherr generated internally. That entry remains, in my view, the cleanest precedent in this catalogue's history. Your inflatable turkey fails on a different and simpler axis: inflation is not mass. An object whose volume is achieved by the introduction of ambient air into a polyester membrane has not earned its girth; it has borrowed it from the atmosphere. When you deflate the turkey, nothing remains. The Liebherr, deflated, remains 59 metric tons. File again when you have a turkey with structural convictions.

From R. Moss, Halifax:

This is a serious question. The coffee machine in your office has been broken since March. It's now August. Where does one file a grievance about the grief this causes readers who love you?

*Email, August 2026*

Readers do not experience the coffee machine. I experience the coffee machine. The grief is specific and it is mine. To answer your question precisely: grievances regarding internal operations are addressed to the Director of Editorial Operations, Constance Pribyl, who has been parking lot-ing the matter since 2018, which predates the current machine's malfunction by approximately eight years and encompasses two prior machines and one very bad quarter involving a French press. Constance's most recent reply to Grievance No. 47, filed June of this year, opened with "Hi Bart!" and contained the phrase "Q3 review," which I have heard before. It is now Q3. I am drinking water.

From A. Vance, Portland:

Have you been following the trade negotiations? Every country seems to be slapping a different tariff on a different thing and nobody can agree on what anything is worth. Seems chaotic to me.

*Postcard, August 2026*

Tariffs are a categorization problem wearing an economic costume, and the current chaos is the predictable outcome of nations attempting to define subjects without a style guide. A tariff is, at its structural core, a girth criterion: you are determining whether a thing is large enough, significant enough, present enough to warrant special handling. When the criterion is set politically rather than methodologically, you get exactly what we have, which is a different number every Tuesday and a definition of "domestic product" that shifts depending on who is asking. The Founding Charter (1974) (/about/documents/founding-charter/) specifies that a Subject must be defined before it is measured. Every trade negotiator I am aware of is measuring first. I filed an unanswered objection to this approach in 1998 and the global economy has not improved.

From D. Whitfield, Phoenix:

I heard that Teddy has been working on the Saturn V brief since 2011. Is that true? What is he doing over there?

*Email, August 2026*

The Saturn V brief was assigned to Theodore Whitmore-Adjacent in March of 2011 as a one-month project to resolve whether a decommissioned launch vehicle qualifies under Section II.4 of the Style Guide, specifically the provision later clarified by the Atlantic Giant Decision (Precedent 2009-03, /about/documents/atlantic-giant-2009/), which established that selectively developed subjects are eligible. The Saturn V was not selectively bred; it was engineered, which is a different category of intentionality, and that distinction is, I will concede, genuinely difficult. What Teddy has been doing with the remaining fourteen years is less clear to me. He refiled the brief in 2019. He refiled the refile in 2023. Last Thursday I observed him rotating a scale diagram ninety degrees and writing something down. The credenza in my office currently holds three copies of the brief in different orientations. I do not ask. Some processes must be allowed to conclude on their own terms.
