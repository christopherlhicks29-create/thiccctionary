Extensive and Intensive: A Distinction the Style Guide Requires.

A cargo freighter and a fighter aircraft arrived in the same week. Bartholomew Pruss has reviewed both, and finds they share a word but not a property.

Published Monday, September 28, 2026

Two Entries, One Unresolved Question

Two entries arrived at the offices this week that, taken separately, present no particular difficulty. Taken together, they constitute what I will call, with some reluctance, a structural problem. The Thiccc Cargo Freighter is large in the manner the catalogue was built to receive: slow, displacing an extraordinary volume of water, committed to its dimensions in a way that admits no ambiguity. The Thiccc Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor is large in a different manner entirely. It is dense, compact relative to its power output, and moves at speeds exceeding Mach 2. The question the Style Guide has not answered, and which I am now required to answer in its absence, is whether girth in motion is the same property as girth at rest.

I have been at this publication for seventeen years. I catalogued ships originally. Bertram hired me in person. I know what a large vessel looks like from a pier. I stood at the harbor on Saturday morning and thought about this. I did not write anything down. I am writing it down now.

What the Freighter Asks of the Assessor

The cargo freighter is, by any reasonable measure, a heritage subject. The classification criteria are settled. Gross tonnage is the governing metric. The hull dimensions are verifiable. A fully laden Panamax freighter displaces approximately 65,000 tonnes of water. Its beam runs to around 32 metres. These numbers do not require interpretation. They require a moment of stillness to absorb.

The object does not move quickly enough to complicate the measurement. You can walk around it, if the dock permits, and it will still be there when you return. This is, per Style Guide section II.3, the preferred condition for primary assessment: the object should not be moving faster than the assessor. I have filed no objections to this entry. The records desk has begun leaving my forms in a separate tray, so the absence of a filing here is worth noting explicitly.

What the Raptor Does to the Framework

The F-22 Raptor is 18.9 metres long, with a wingspan of 13.56 metres, and an empty weight of approximately 19,700 kilograms. These are not large numbers by the catalogue's standards. The Stack of Pancakes, which I reviewed four days ago with significant reservations about the durability of the entry over time, presents a more immediately legible girth profile than the Raptor does on paper. And yet the Raptor is unambiguously thiccc. The question is why.

My position, arrived at after two days of consideration and one conversation I will not be repeating with the Junior Cataloguer, is this: the Raptor's girth is a function of density rather than dimension. The aircraft carries two Pratt and Whitney F119 engines, each producing 35,000 pounds of thrust with afterburner. The fuselage is built around that thrust. Every structural decision, the blended wing body, the internal weapons bays that preserve the aerodynamic profile, the weight distribution across the airframe, exists because the object must contain an extraordinary amount of force within a fixed envelope. This is girth under compression. The Style Guide does not name this category. It should.

The Proposed Notation, Filed for the Record

I want to distinguish between what I will call extensive girth and intensive girth. The cargo freighter is extensively thiccc: its mass and volume are large in absolute terms, distributed across a form that makes no attempt at concealment. The Raptor is intensively thiccc: its mass is moderate, but the concentration of structural commitment within that envelope is extraordinary. The girth is, in the most literal sense, internal.

The Junior Cataloguer disputes this distinction on the grounds that it introduces subjectivity into what he prefers to describe as a 'dimensional discipline.' He said this on Thursday, in those words, while standing next to the credenza. I did not respond at the time. I have drafted a proposed amendment to section IV.1 of the Style Guide and left it on Margie's desk. It has been four days. Both entries are approved. The Raptor is approved with a notation: it is the first formally identified instance of intensive girth in the current classification framework, and should be treated as a precedent entry pending amendment.

The editorial board recommends 32 metres of beam and Mach 2 be considered, separately, for no less than four minutes each. Begin with the freighter. It will wait.
