# The World Cup Is Now Too Large to Be Hosted. It Can Only Be Regionally Absorbed.

**THE THICCC BEAT:** *the desk reacts to the news. The Publisher, writing from the deck of the Margaret IV, considers a tournament that has outgrown the concept of a host.*

In 1930, the World Cup was thirteen teams in Uruguay. One country. One month. A trophy you could carry in one hand while shaking hands with the other. It was, by every measure this publication respects, svelte.

I have watched institutions grow for most of my life. Ships, ledgers, archives, my own masthead. Growth is ordinary. What concluded on the 19th of July was not ordinary. Forty-eight teams. One hundred and four matches, the most ever staged, a figure certified by the record-keepers at Guinness, who do not certify casually. Three sovereign nations, pressed into joint service because no single one of them could contain the thing. Attendance reported north of six million, which is not a crowd. That is a migration.

Understand what happened here. The tournament did not visit a country this summer. It settled over a continent the way weather does. Mexico City to Vancouver to New Jersey, one competition, wearing three nations at once, the first in history to require this. The final itself, Spain over Argentina, one goal to nil, was almost slender by comparison. A single goal. After a hundred and three matches of accumulation, the object came to rest on the narrowest possible point, the way a container ship rests on a keel.

Some at this desk will grumble that a tournament is an event, not an object, and events are outside our remit. I am the Publisher. I sign the remit. Anything that takes three countries to hold has earned its filing.

**RULING: Thiccc. The first tournament to require a continent. The 2030 edition is already assigned to three more nations on two continents, and this desk will be watching its figure.**
